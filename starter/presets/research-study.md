# Preset — research & study

For students, postgraduates, academics and anyone studying seriously on their own. Used once by init-library; later by tune-up.

## Words
- Work item: **project** (a thesis, paper, study or report) or **course** (something you are taking). `kind: project` or `kind: course`

## Areas
- `projects/` — one item per piece of research or writing
- `courses/` — one item per course or subject being studied
- `reading/` — one item per topic or body of literature, holding reading notes
- `methods/` — how-to notes: techniques, tools, analysis recipes
- `ideas/` — questions worth asking one day

## Work item sections
Project: The question (one sentence, and why it matters) · Approach (method, data or sources) · Plan and milestones (what · by when) · What I know so far · Argument or outline as it stands · People (supervisor, collaborators) · Current state · Open questions · Files · Related
Course: Overview (what, who teaches, how assessed) · Schedule and key dates (deadlines, exams) · Topics (list with a self-rating: new / shaky / solid) · Current state · Open questions · Files

## Routines

### reading-note
One note per source in the item's `notes/`:
- Full reference, and where to find it
- The question it asks and its main claim, in two sentences of your own
- How it gets there — method, data, kind of argument
- The evidence that matters, with page numbers
- Limits and what you doubt
- How it connects — agrees with, contradicts, extends which other notes (link them)
- Quotations worth keeping, exact and with page numbers, clearly marked as quotations
- What it changes for your own project

### literature-map
A note per topic: the main positions, who holds each, where they disagree, what nobody has addressed — updated after every few reading notes. The gap it reveals feeds The question.

### weekly-review
What was read and written · what moved against the milestones · what is blocked and who can unblock it · three things for next week as todos with dates.

### supervision-meeting
Before: a short agenda — progress since last time, two or three questions, what you need decided. After: a meeting note the same day with the advice given, decisions, and action items for both sides.

### writing-pipeline
Per piece: outline agreed → rough draft (complete, not polished) → structural revision → line editing → references and formatting check → submitted. Status and next step kept in Current state; drafts for readers go in `outputs/`.

### revision-plan
For an exam: topics listed with self-rating · dates worked back from the exam · short sessions that revisit each topic at widening intervals · practice questions under timed conditions · after each session, re-rate the topic.

## Worked example
Project "Example — literature review on remote work and productivity": question stated, three milestones with dates, one reading note written in full in the format above, a first literature map with two positions; todos "Find five more sources on measurement", "Draft outline", "Book supervision meeting".

## Care points
- Keep the user's own words and a source's words apart, always: quotations exact, marked and referenced; paraphrases referenced too.
- Never invent a reference, a page number or a finding. If a source was not actually read, say so; if unsure a source exists, say that it must be checked.
- The assistant helps think, structure and critique; what counts as acceptable help with assessed work is set by the user's institution — raise it once if the user asks for text to submit.
- Research data about people stays out of notes unless anonymised.
